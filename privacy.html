Mountain Mogul privacy policy

Last updated October 29, 2026

Mountain Mogul is a free browser ski resort game at mtmogul.com, made by Phil at MossQuake. This policy explains the game’s data practices. You can read it without an account.

At a glance

  • You can play without providing an email address. Guest play still creates an online guest account when the game connects.
  • Your resort is saved in your browser and can also be saved online. Resorts start public; you can change that in Profile.
  • The game sends technical error reports and stores feedback you submit to help the developer fix problems.
  • We do not sell your information or use advertising cookies.

Data we use and why

  • Guest accounts and sign-in. When the game connects, it creates or reuses an account identified by an ID, including for guest players. If you sign in with email or Google, we receive your email address and, from Google, account information such as your name and profile picture. Your sign-in name, email and Google picture are not displayed to other players. Email is used to identify your account and send sign-in links you request.
  • Resort saves and browser storage. Saves include your mountain layout, buildings, finances, progress, resort and creator names, game settings, and a scenic image of your mountain. Browser storage also keeps sign-in sessions, display preferences, selected resorts, invitation information and queued diagnostic reports. Online saves let an account continue on another device. Clearing browser data alone does not delete online saves.
  • Feedback you submit. Your message is stored with your account ID and game context, including game version, resort name, mountain, in-game date, cash, resort size, screen size, touch support and browser information. Feedback is for the developer, not a public player message board.
  • Automatic diagnostics. Errors, failed launches and unexpected page closures can generate reports with an error message or stack trace, game version, browser/device information, screen size and game context. Reports can wait in browser storage and be sent on the next connection. These help diagnose crashes and performance problems.
  • Community activity. Resort visits, likes, roadmap votes and update reactions are stored with account identifiers and timestamps where applicable. They support counts, rankings, voting and preventing duplicate actions. The developer uses account and save activity to understand how people use the game.
  • Friend invitations. If you use an invitation link, the game records the invitation code and links the inviting and invited accounts. Resort creation, qualifying operating days and reward claims are recorded to determine eligibility and prevent duplicate rewards. The inviter can see qualification progress; your sign-in email is not shown in the invitation progress card.

What is public and who can see it

Resorts start public, including guest resorts shown as “Guest”. Public resorts can appear on leaderboards and Browse. Other players can see the resort name, creator name you choose, statistics, mountain image and a read-only copy of the resort. Public creator profiles can group your public resorts together. Use a nickname if you do not want your real name displayed.

Change your resort’s visibility in Profile, sharing and sign-in from the game menu. A private resort is hidden from other players. The developer can inspect a read-only copy of public or private resorts to understand problems; this does not change your resort. Feedback and diagnostic reports are not displayed in the public game.

If you share an invitation or resort image through another app, that app handles the content you send under its own policies. Do not put personal information into public resort or creator names.

Service providers and browser requests

The game is hosted on GitHub Pages. Supabase provides accounts, online saves and other game records. Google provides optional Google sign-in and web fonts. Your browser contacts these services to load the game and use their features; these requests expose network information such as your IP address to the service receiving them.

Provider information: GitHub privacy statement, Supabase privacy policy, and Google privacy policy.

We do not sell your information, share it with advertisers, or use advertising or cross-site tracking cookies. The account IDs, sign-in storage and activity records described above are used for the game and its operation.

Your choices, retention and deletion

  • Play as a guest to avoid providing an email address. Guest play is not an offline-only or no-data mode.
  • Make a resort private in Profile to remove it from public browsing and leaderboards. This does not delete it.
  • Delete a resort from its title-screen card: choose More options (⋯), then Delete this mountain. Connect to the server to delete an online save. Deleting a resort does not also delete your account or every feedback, diagnostic or reward record associated with it.
  • Clear this site’s browser storage using your browser settings to remove local saves, preferences and sign-in sessions. Local progress can be lost. Online data must be removed separately.
  • Request account or other data deletion through the contact options below. For an account request, use feedback while connected to that account so the developer can identify it. Do not post your email address, passwords or sign-in links in a public GitHub issue.

There is no published fixed automatic deletion period for all game records. Online saves and account-linked records can remain until deleted or removed by the developer. Removing a resort alone can leave operational records, including invitation reward history used to prevent duplicate payments.

For schools, parents and students

No email address is required to try the game, but guest accounts, online saves and automatic diagnostics still apply. Public resort browsing and player-created resort and creator names are part of the game. Students should avoid entering personal details in names or feedback.

This page describes the game’s data practices; it is not a school-approval or compliance certification. Schools should review these practices and public features under their own requirements before allowing student use. Questions about data handling can be sent to the developer below.

Contact the developer

Open Mountain Mogul and choose Feedback & feature ideas from the resort menu, or Feedback on the title screen, to send a private message to the developer. Include “Privacy question” or “Data deletion request” and explain what you need. Never send passwords or sign-in links.

You can also open an issue on the game’s GitHub page, but GitHub issues are public. Use the in-game feedback route for personal information or account-specific requests.
